# NO MONEY DOWN

> You and the boys bought a $30,500 motorhome with no money down.
> Payments are due every night. None of you have jobs.

A 1–6 player **first-person co-op road trip**, built for proximity voice and
bad decisions. Drive the Slopmaster 9000 across five days of procedurally
generated road (green meadows, golden farmland, a snowbound pass, red canyon
country, open desert), scavenge fragile junk, climb, winch, shove, gamble the
shared bank account, and pay the Repo Man by midnight, every night. Miss one
payment and he takes the RV.

## The RV takes a beating

The RV has a **condition**, shown in the top bar once it's hurt. Crashes dent it: run it into the ranger
gate at 8 m/s and it's about 18% gone, and everyone hears who was driving ("CRUNCH. Sam put the RV into
the ranger gate: -18%"). A Grumphorn's charge, a rock landing on the roof and a ringtail breaking out
through a window dent it too. Worn down it gets slower (smoking, then limping, when a knock stalls the
engine), a big hit throws the door open, and at 0% it **breaks down**: everybody out, and hold `E` at the
engine hatch on its back until it coughs back to life (one of you takes 8 seconds, three take under 3).
**Gus** fixes it by the RV lot in town at night, once the Repo Man is paid: 25% a press, for cash or on
tomorrow's payment. It never ends a trip by itself: only a missed payment does.

Gus also **upgrades** the RV, from the chalkboard of parts by his bench (`E` on it opens his catalogue),
at night once the day is paid, paid from the shared bank, and fitted for the rest of the trip:

| Upgrade | Price | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Reinforced Frame | $450 | crashes, Grumphorn hits and falling rock cost the RV 30% less |
| Heavy-Duty Winch | $350 | a 60 m cable (from 42) and half again the pull |
| Big-Block V8 | $600 | a quarter more pull up the grades, a top end of 45 mph (from 38) |
| Mud Tires | $250 | twice the grip in mud and half the drag |
| Locker Padlock | $150 | ringtails can't pry the RV's locker open |
| Nitrous Kit | $400 | `N` at the wheel: 4 s of a hard shove and 9 mph more, three shots a day |

Every dollar there is a dollar short of the next payment: a crew with a good first night can buy speed,
a crew scraping by can't.

## Buried metal

The general store keeps **hardware** on a rack out front, right of the door under the HARDWARE board (`E` on
it): a **metal detector** ($250) and a **shovel** ($100), set down by the rack when you buy them, any time of
day. Every day's road has metal
buried along it, half of it near the stops and the richest down the side roads: hold the detector and
it beeps faster (and the signal meter under the crosshair fills) as its coil nears a cache; the meter
says DIG HERE over one. Whoever holds the shovel digs where they look with `LMB` (hold it to keep digging):
each spadeful flies onto a pile beside the hole as it goes down, and six strokes reach the bottom. Under
a cache there's a **chest**: hold `E` on it to **pull** it up (mash `E` to yank it up quicker; two or
three pulling go faster), and it lands on the lip. `E` opens it and what's inside pops out (scrap iron,
copper wire, a silver or gold nugget, a jar of old coins, a rusty lockbox), loot like any other for Ed,
and so is the old chest. Bare ground digs down to nothing. One holds the detector, one digs. Ed won't buy
the gear back; set it down in the RV and it goes in the **locker** with the loot (what's left on the road
stays there).

## The RV's locker

Anything set down in the RV, loot and gear alike, goes in its **locker**, out of the way and safe from the
road's knocks. `E` on the locker opens the list of everything in it (the gear by kind, the loot dearest first)
and takes out whatever you pick, into your hands. Ed's loading zone buys the locker's loot with the rest of
the load and leaves the gear in it; it all rides into the next day.

## Dynamite

The hardware rack sells **dynamite** too ($60 a stick). Hold one and `LMB` lights the fuse: four seconds,
sparking and hissing, then it goes off wherever it is, in your hands included, so `RMB` throws it. Out on
the road there's rock **seamed with metal** (copper's green, silver's white, gold's yellow), too big to
move: a stick beside one blows it apart into **ore** for Ed (and rubble). A stick by the narrows' boulder
blows that to bits too, a way through without the winch, but it brings down any loose rock on the rim.
Close in it knocks you out cold, further out off your feet; it throws loose things about, dents the RV
if it's near, and sets off any other stick lying close. Not in town: the sheriff'd lock you up.

## Hats, outfits and paint

Out front of the general store, left of the door, is a **clothes rail** (`E` on it): nine hats, the six outfits
(pauldrons, emblem, belt and bracers) and eight tabard dyes, $40 to $220 each from the shared bank. Gus
also **paints the RV** (six colours, $250 to $350), at night once the day is paid, like his parts. What
anyone buys is the **crew's**, for good: it goes into the save of everyone aboard, so whoever brings it on
a later trip brings it for the whole crew, and anyone can wear it. You change only where it's sold (the
clothes at the rail, the paint at Gus's), in any town on any day. Your name tag and party portrait keep
your own colour, so the crew still knows who's who.

## Beat your time, beat your haul

Every trip is timed and counted. **Road time** is the clock at each day's payment less 6:00, added up over
the five days (pay at 2 PM every day and it's 30:00 of real time); the **biggest haul** is everything you
pawned. The **Garage** (the menu, the Esc card, or `G`) keeps your fastest payoff, your best payment time
for each day, your biggest haul, your cleanest delivery, a tally for every crew you've finished a trip
with, and lifetime counters. Records need you aboard from the first morning to the end; a trip from a
typed seed is practice and counts only on that seed's board. The save is yours, on your own PC (or in
your browser), with Export and Import in the Garage to move it.

## Wildlife

From day 2 a **Grumphorn**, a one-ton shaggy beast with curled horns and a brass cowbell, sleeps on the
road past one of the obstacles (two on day 5). A yellow sign warns you, the navigator's map marks it, and
you hear it snoring. Walk up to it standing, sprint near it, drive past fast, stand up in the RV going
by, or honk, and it wakes, glares, fixes a line and charges down it. The first person in its horns goes
flying (knocked out: pick them up within 20 s or it's the doctor's bill); anyone else it brushes stumbles.

It has no health bar, only **patience**. Sneak past crouched, creep the RV by, or wear it out: throw junk
at it, shove it, ram it with the RV (everyone crouch to brace first), winch it. Each charge and each toss
costs it patience too. When it runs out, it drops its cowbell (worth $205 to $320 at the pawn shop) and
walks off the road for good. It never touches the bank, leaves alone anyone who is down (and whoever
kneels by them), and knocks out at most one player per charge. There's a heap of stones by its warning
sign for throwing, and if two of you shove its side together while it sleeps, it tips over: it lies there
kicking for 12 seconds, long enough to get the RV past, and then it's up and not best pleased.

Every day, **Ringtails** (raccoon-sized bandits with black masks and ringed tails) live in dens beside
some of the stops: one den on day 1, three by day 5, marked with paw prints on the map. They sneak up on
loot nobody is watching, grab it and run it home to their pile. Stand facing your loot and they keep
their distance. Catch one and it drops what it took (from day 2 it bites if you hold it alone: hold it
with a friend); shove it or hit it with something and it's stunned; honk and they scatter; run one over
and it's squashed flat for a while. A den only steals so much a day, and everything it took is still
there at its den: go and get it back. The tracker says what they're holding of yours.

From day 2 they get bolder. Leave the RV parked with its door open and nobody near it, and one may hop in,
pry the locker open and run off with the best thing it can carry: shut the door or stay by it. Drive off with one inside and it lets go and jumps out. Walk up to a den and they come
out to nip you: you stumble and drop whatever you carry (they leave alone anyone kneeling by a friend who
is down, and anyone up out of reach).

From day 3, **loose rock** sits on the rim above the narrows (the boulder and the ranger gate). A yellow
LOOSE ROCK sign warns you and the navigator's map says "HONK FIRST". When the RV gets close, or someone
shoves the boulder, the rim rumbles and the rocks come down: one still falling fast knocks out whoever it
hits, a rolling one bowls you over. Honk at the sign and it all comes down before you get there. Afterwards
the rocks are just heavy stones, good for throwing at a Grumphorn.

And mind your aim: junk you throw at a friend knocks them over (never out) and they drop what they hold.
Everyone hears who did it, and the end of the run counts the beanings.

Day 5 has **the Hoard**: one den (the junkyard's, when the day has one) where six of them live (four
against a lone player, five against two), on a pile of the week's best stash (about $2,500 of it, marked
on the map in red). Get it out past all those teeth and the balloon payment looks a lot closer. The store helps: a **hard hat** ($80) turns your next
knockout into a knock, and a **cheese wheel** ($30) is bait they can't resist: put it where you want them,
away from your loot or out past their den.

and town. You never see the same place twice in a run.

## Why this game, and not the last one

This repo's first attempt (THE SLOP PIT) was six two-minute homage minigames
on a 2D plane with one verb (dash). It wasn't fun, and **[docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md)**
explains why and what replaced it. The short version: every friendslop hit is
*one deep physics verb + proximity voice + shared stakes under a clock*, so
this game has one spine (the RV road trip) and steals one verb from each
classic:

| From | We took |
|---|---|
| **PEAK** | Climb any steep surface on stamina. Carried weight shrinks your bar. Lunge for holds. Knockouts, friends picking you up, campfire nights. |
| **R.V. There Yet?** | A shared physics vehicle you all ride inside. A front winch you carry to an anchor and reel in. |
| **Gamble With Your Friends** | One shared bank account, one debt, a nightly payment, physical casino games anyone can bet. |
| **We Were Here** | Information only one person has: the paper map, the gate code on the rim, the driver's blind spots. Walkie-talkies cost money. |
| **REPO / Lethal Company** | Fragile physics loot that loses value when it gets bumped. Haul it back to the vehicle. A quota collector. |

## Play it

**The desktop build (the one for Steam):**

```bash
npm install
npm run desktop    # with Steam running: friends join by trip code or Steam invite
```

One person clicks **Host Game** and reads out the 4-digit trip code at the top
right of their screen (it's on the `Esc` menu too). Friends type it under
**Join Game**, and their game finds the trip through Steam. Steam invites work
too: **Invite Friends** (press `Esc`), or **Join Game** on the host in the
Steam friends list. The host's PC runs the trip, and everyone talks to it over
Steam, so there is nothing to port-forward. Without Steam running it plays
solo. How the Steam build works, and how to ship it:
[docs/STEAM.md](docs/STEAM.md).

**The browser build (LAN and development):**

```bash
npm start          # → http://localhost:3000
```

One person clicks **Host Game** and sends the 4-digit trip code; everyone else
types it under **Join Game**. Click the window to grab the mouse; **F1** shows controls, `Esc`
frees the mouse and offers Leave Trip. To replay the same roads, put the same
number in "trip seed". A dropped connection gets its seat back if it returns
within 90 s.

**The first load takes a while.** Every texture is painted and every mesh is
built in your browser. Background workers paint what they can while the
loading bar is up, your browser keeps the painted textures (IndexedDB) so
the next visit decodes them instead, and the next day gets ready during the
night. On a machine with no GPU (software rendering on 4 CPU cores), from
"Host Game": the first visit draws its first frames after about 23 s, a
returning visit after about 22 s (its world is built in about 6 s), and a
later day takes 9–14 s. Most of the rest on that machine is the software
renderer drawing its first frames; a real graphics card does that much
faster, but we haven't measured it. Use a browser with hardware acceleration
on.

| | |
|---|---|
| `WASD` `Shift` `Space` `C` | walk · sprint · jump · crouch (in the RV: **brace**, a crash can't knock you out) |
| `E` | **pick up** loot (`E` again sets it down; `Q` drops it too); use: driver seat, door, winch, the RV's locker (the list of what's in it: take out what you want; anything set down in the RV goes in it), the RV's outside (**push** it: hold `W`, `E` lets go), pawn bell, a casino game's button (sit down to play it), keypad, bunk (lies you down; `E` or walking gets you up). Hold it on a knocked-out friend to pick them up. |
| **hold `LMB`** | **climb** any steep rock or wall (`Space` = lunge) |
| `RMB` · wheel | throw (empty-handed: **shove**) · reach |
| driving | `WASD` · `Space` brake · `R` reel the winch · `H` horn · `N` nitrous (with Gus's kit) · `E` get up (the RV keeps rolling) |
| `MMB` · `1-6` | ping · emotes (at a casino game the number keys play it: 1-4 the stake, 5 deal/hit/roll/pull; blackjack 6 stand, 7 double, 8 split; craps 7 odds behind the line; at the Derby 5-8 a ticket on ringtail 1-4) |
| `V` · `M` · `T` | push-to-talk · mute · walkie-talkie |

**A day:** each morning picks a driver and a navigator at random (whoever
has done a job less is likelier to get it), locked once the RV leaves camp
(if one drops out or goes AFK, anyone stands in till they're back) → leave
camp → follow the road (the driver can't see
the map: the navigator reads it out, matching the odometer to its 100 m
marks; alone, you drive without it) → deal with
what's broken (winch up the grade, push out of the mud (`E` on the RV's back, hold `W`: it takes two), shove or winch the
boulder, crack the ranger gate) and with what lives there (see Wildlife below)
→ detour to stops for loot → town: pawn it,
gamble it, pay the Repo Man → campfire, store, everyone in a bunk → next day.
Five days, and the payment due at midnight grows each day ($1,000, $2,500,
$4,500, $7,500, then the $15,000 balloon). Pay the balloon on day 5 and you own
the RV. Come up short on any night and he takes the RV: the trip is over.

## Music

Every place has its own tune, played by a synthesized western band: a Travis-picked guitar and harmonica on
the Westmeadow Road, a banjo-and-fiddle hoedown through the fields, a lonely whistled waltz on the snowy pass,
slide guitar over a twangy baritone in the badlands, and a spaghetti western (tremolo guitar, castanets, a
choir, the whistle) into Lost Wages. Under the payment, a lullaby by the fire.

It follows what's happening near you: creep past a sleeping Grumphorn and the strings start to tremble and
a heartbeat comes in; wake it and the timpani roll; when it charges a whip cracks and the band breaks into a
gallop. Ringtails sneaking up bring pizzicato on tiptoe and a jaw harp. In the last hour before midnight a
clock ticks under everything, and the church bell starts to toll. The tune plays a minute or so and then rests,
leaves the floor to the RV's radio and the casino's band, and ducks whenever anyone talks. Settings: Music
while you play turns it off (the radio and the casino's band stay).

## Voice

Everyone is in voice from the moment they're in the game: the browser asks for the mic once, and
without one you still hear everyone near you (your frame says you've no mic on; click the mic at the
bottom left to try again). It's **proximity chat**: each voice is Opus audio sent
over the game's own connection, and the host passes it only to the players who
can hear it, who hear it through a 3D HRTF panner at the speaker's head. It's at full volume within
4 m and gone by 25 m (everyone in the RV hears each other; across a stop or a town, nobody does), gets
muffled when terrain or the RV is between you, and sounds face-down when
someone's knocked out. **Walkie-talkies** ($150 each at the general store) reach
anyone else who owns one, at any range, through a crunchy radio filter. Hold
`T`. Play on the game's voice, not a call: who hears you is half the game.
How loud each friend is to you is on their frame at the top left: press Esc for the mouse, then their
slider and mute button are under their name (kept by name, trip to trip). `M` mutes your own mic.

Nothing is peer-to-peer, so nobody's router gets in the way and nobody sees
anyone's IP address. In a browser, mics need a secure context (https or
localhost): for LAN play put the server behind any TLS tunnel (Tailscale,
cloudflared, ngrok) or reverse proxy. The desktop build doesn't have this
problem.

## How it's built

```
shared/   world.js     seeded deterministic leg generator: biome, terrain, road, obstacles, stops,
                       town, decor, and every collider (trees, rocks, shelves, yard props)
          rv.js        the RV's collider layout (server body, client copy and renderer all use it)
          loot.js      loot catalog: mass, value, fragility, carry class
desktop/  main.cjs     the desktop app (Electron): the window, the game server in its own process,
                       Steam (lobbies, invites, rich presence); steam/: friends' traffic over Steam P2P
server/   host.js      every connection (the host's page, friends over Steam, browsers): rooms, kept
                       seats, rate limits, the voice relay; index.js runs it for the browser build
          sim.js       Rapier 3D physics: heightfield, RV on raycast wheels, loot, grab springs,
                       push intents, winch cable + anchors + reel, impact damage, run-over checks
          room.js      the run: days, clock, shared bank, pawn, casino, store, Repo Man, KO bills
          casino.js    the casino: a game each on one bank (blackjack, craps, double or nothing, the Derby)
          blackjack.js the blackjack table: one round for every seat (the deal, turns, double, split, the dealer)
client/   player.js    first-person controller: walk/sprint/jump, stamina climbing, riding the
                       moving RV (RV-local coordinates), lurching when it brakes, knockouts
          phys.js      local Rapier world of fixed copies (moved each frame), for the controller
          paint/       the texture painter: core.js (brushes, blotches, strokes, seamless blur)
                       plus one family per domain (terrain, nature, architecture, vehicle,
                       characters, props, roadside, ui)
          texprep.js · texcache.js · paint/worker.js   textures ready before a day builds: paint
                       workers, the browser's texture cache (IndexedDB), the night's prefetch
          gfx.js · atmosphere.js     lights, sky, fog, clouds, horizon rings, day/night per biome
          terrain3d.js               splatted painted ground, cliffs, ground clutter
          nature3d.js                oaks, pines, palms, hoodoos, buttes, arches, boulders, cacti
          town3d.js · town_build.js · town_kit.js · town_yard.js   five town styles, interiors, NPCs
          roadside3d.js              the stops, gates, camp, anchors, junk, the dino
          rv3d.js · props3d.js · people.js · labels.js   the RV, loot, characters, painted signs/HUD
          voice.js     Opus voice over the game connection + spatial audio · sfx.js: synthesized sound
          score.js · western.js   the score: each biome's tune and the layers that follow the game, on a
                       synthesized western band (also the trailer's) · music.js: the RV's radio, the casino's band
          ambience.js  the wind and each biome's wildlife
tools/    docshots.mjs  the README screenshots · scene.mjs  named in-game viewpoints per day
          gallery.mjs   every texture, tiled to show seams · preview.mjs  single 3D assets
          drive-test.mjs  headless autopilot down a leg (RV handling)
          loadtime.mjs  load-time numbers · texhash.mjs · texgame.mjs · texfaults.mjs  the
                       texture cache and paint workers give the same pixels, under faults too
          joinrace.mjs  loot that breaks while a day loads: clients still match the server
          dayrace.mjs   a day that changes again mid-load: nothing of the old day leaks into
                       the new one (npm run test:race runs both)
```

- **Server-authoritative world, client-authoritative bodies.** The server
  simulates the RV, the loot and the winch at 60 Hz and snapshots at 20 Hz.
  Each client moves its own body through a local copy of the same seeded
  world with Rapier's character controller, so there's no input lag, and
  reports its pose. A grab is a spring force from your hand on the server, so
  two people carrying one safe just works.
- **Zero assets.** Every mesh, texture and sound is generated. The world is
  regenerated from a seed on every machine, so no geometry crosses the
  network. Trees, rocks and props are drawn from the colliders world gen
  made for them (an oak's bole, a hoodoo's column, a boulder's top), so the
  big things you see are the things you bump into.

## Tests

```bash
npm test
```

- `tools/namecheck.mjs` fails if another game's place names or a real brand
  get into the text players can see (the store and the Steam agreement need
  everything shipped to be ours).
- `test/logic.mjs` plays a whole run over real WebSockets with two fake
  players and real physics (71 checks). It drives, carries and throws a vase
  (it chips), cracks the ranger gate, winches the RV up the grade, pawns a
  TV, plays blackjack and double-or-nothing at once out of one bank,
  buys a walkie, pays the Repo Man, sleeps into day 2, gets billed for a KO,
  gets revived for free, gets run over, misses a payment and is repo'd on the
  spot, starts over, and wins on day 5.
- `test/server.mjs` checks what keeps an evening going when connections
  misbehave: kept seats, malformed frames, floods, a different build, silent
  connections, the goodbye when the server shuts down; and every trip's
  summary reaching whoever was on it (held for a dropped player till they're
  back, and only for who rode), and what counts as practice.
- `test/rv.mjs` checks the RV's condition in process: what a crash costs and
  what must never count as one (keyboard driving, a handbrake stop with safes
  aboard, teleports, the tow off a steep bank, the winch), the winch hauling
  three safes up the steepest grade with nobody driving, the bands, the stall,
  the roadside fix, Gus, and that it never ends a trip. `node tools/rvsoak.mjs` is its soak: a
  keyboard-style driver does all of it on 12 seeds x 5 days, plus every
  stop's apron, and prints every crash window for tuning.
- `test/steamnet.mjs` plays over a stand-in for Steam (`test/fakesteam/`):
  invites, the version check, a dropped link coming back to its seat, voice,
  packet loss, the host quitting or crashing.
- `test/music.mjs` plays the score offline in Chromium: every biome's tune renders without
  clipping, within a few dB of the others; the layers that answer the wildlife and the clock make
  sound; the plucked strings are in tune; and the score's levels follow what's going on (a charge in
  at once, the tune giving way to the radio and the town). `MUSIC_DEMO=dir` also writes them as WAVs.
- `test/smoke-browser.mjs` runs two real Chromium clients in one room. They
  join through the menu and see each other. One drives while the other rides
  inside, and the passenger stays glued in on both screens. They carry a TV,
  climb a canyon wall, read the map to the driver (only the day's navigator
  takes it, only while the driver drives, and the driver's screen never shows
  it), play the casino (blackjack at one seat and Double or Nothing at the same
  time, then a Derby ticket each; the score gives the floor to the casino's band), hear the meadow's
  tune and the RV's radio, talk over proximity voice, drop and get their seat back. The
  test fails on any page error.
- `npm run test:desktop` drives the real desktop app (needs a display, or
  `xvfb-run`): the locked-down shell, a trip, Quit, and two copies of the app
  joining each other over the stand-in Steam.
- After `npm run dist`, `node tools/depotcheck.mjs` checks the packaged
  folders before a Steam upload.

`FRIENDSLOP_FAST=1` speeds the clock up, and `FRIENDSLOP_TEST=1` enables test
levers (teleport the RV, set the clock). Don't set either in production.
